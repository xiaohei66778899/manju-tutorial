# Vibe Coding for Video: How Claude Can Direct an Entire Film

Vibe coding for video works the same way as regular vibe coding, except the "code" is a film. You talk about mood, story, and intent. Your agent interprets that brief, plans the production, and drives the tools that generate images, clips, and the final edit.

## What Directing Means When Claude Is in the Loop

Traditional AI video tools ask you to write a prompt, click generate, and hope for the best. That works for a single shot. It falls apart for anything with a story arc, recurring characters, or a consistent visual world.

Vibe coding for video inverts the workflow. You give the agent a creative brief — sometimes a sentence, sometimes a paragraph — and it treats that like a director's treatment. From there, it structures the work:

### 1. Interpret the Brief

Extract genre, tone, pacing, and non-negotiables from what you said — not from generic model habits.

Example: "Two-minute thriller, rainy evening, one woman, inherited Victorian house, envelope that wasn't there yesterday, end on attic door moving" gives the agent a genre, cast size, prop, and final image to aim at.

### 2. Break It into Scenes

Each scene gets one job: establish, escalate, reveal, resolve. Reject scene lists where one beat tries to "find the letter AND run upstairs AND see the ghost."

### 3. Maintain Continuity

Characters, wardrobe, locations, and lighting stay tied to reference assets — not re-described from memory each turn. Your mini web series barista keeps the same apron color because her character bible ID is attached to every storyboard panel.

### 4. Select Tools and Models

Map each beat to the right machinery — stills before motion, references before text-to-video. Storyboard Scene 3 with GPT Image 2; animate with Seedance 2 and the locked sheet attached.

### 5. Review Generated Assets

Compare outputs against the brief and storyboard before assembly — dailies, not premiere. Beat two returns a leather jacket when the bible says wool coat. The agent flags the mismatch before you burn credits on nine more shots.

### 6. Revise Weak Shots

Regenerate specific scenes while the rest of the film stays locked. "Scene 2 too cozy — cooler grade, single lamp, hold on empty chair" updates direction and reruns Scene 2 only.

## A Multi-Turn Conversation Example

**You:** Two-minute thriller. Woman alone in inherited Victorian house, rainy evening. She finds a sealed envelope that was not there yesterday. Minimal dialogue. Tense. End with something moving in the attic.

**Claude:** I will structure this in five scenes: exterior establish, interior calm, envelope discovery, letter beat, attic hook. I will generate a character sheet for "Elena, mid-30s, dark hair, grey sweater" and a location anchor for the living room. Storyboards first with GPT Image 2, then Seedance 2 image-to-video. Proceed?

**You:** Yes. Keep it slow. No voice-over.

**Claude:** First-pass clips are in your project folder. Scene 3 and Scene 5 are strongest. Scene 2 reads too cozy — fireplace glow feels romantic, not uneasy. Scene 4 has two lines of dialogue; your brief said minimal. I can revise 2 and 4 before assembly.

**You:** Make it tenser, reduce dialogue, add a dramatic reveal.

**Claude:** Updating direction:
- Scene 2: Cooler grade, single lamp, remove smile; hold on empty chair in background.
- Scene 4: Cut dialogue; silence, rain on window, slow push-in when she reads the letter.
- Dramatic reveal: Scene 5 intercuts attic door (shadow under crack) with a flash insert from the letter — a surveillance photo of her in this room, dated yesterday.

You never wrote "negative prompt: cheerful lighting." You stated creative intent; the agent translated it across screenplay, generation settings, and selective reruns.

## Weak vs. Strong Creative Direction

**Weak:** One mega-prompt ("Cinematic thriller in a haunted house with plot twist"). No scene list. Different character description every generation. Accept first output. Revision notes like "make it better."

**Strong:** ChatGPT styling narrative, then brief with tone, length, character count, and ending image. Explicit scene beats with one job each. Locked references before motion. Dailies mindset. Revision notes that name what changes narratively and visually.

The gap between an amateur AI clip and a short film you would show someone is usually not the model. It is direction.
